## Workshop Objective

Our objective today is to extract the strategic DNA of Prefera, establishing your brand's foundation as trusted preference infrastructure, from day one.

## Section 1: The Core DNA

### S01 The Spark ("Your Journey")

Baseline: Based on what you've shared, Prefera was born from a professional frustration as much as a personal one. Between you, you've spent your careers translating data protection law into working systems, and you kept seeing the same broken pattern. People are flooded with promotional calls and messages they never asked for, while organizations have no reliable way to know how or when anyone actually wants to hear from them. The breaking point was realising the preference lived nowhere. Every company held a fragment. Nobody held the truth.

Steering Prompt: "Majed, you described a lot of intrusive calls and messages on one side, and on the other, a majority of people who genuinely like loyalty programs and discounts. Nouf, you work where technology meets privacy law every day. Was there one moment, a case, a campaign, a call one of you received, where you both said: this is broken, and we're exactly the two people to fix it?"

### S02 The Breakup

Baseline: The ex is the single-company consent tool. Didomi, Usercentrics and PossibleNOW manage consent inside one brand's walls, so a person has to say no again and again, company by company, and each organization only ever sees its own fragment. The quieter ex is the blunt Do-Not-Contact list, which only understands "no". Prefera's line to the departing client: say it once, and every subscribed organization respects it.

Steering Prompt: "Imagine the marketing director at a large Saudi telecom writing a letter to Didomi that starts: we're leaving you because. You've told us the existing tools are built for individual brands. Finish that letter for us. What's the line that makes them call Prefera instead?"

### S03 The Paradigm Shift

Baseline: The Old Way is preferences scattered across every organization, outreach built on tracking and inferred interest, and an all-or-nothing choice between being bombarded and opting out entirely. The New Reality is one person declaring one set of preferences, organizations verifying before they make contact, and every check leaving a point-in-time record. In your own words, from unwanted marketing to wanted interactions.

Steering Prompt: "Your brief says Prefera is not a Do-Not-Contact registry and not simply a compliance tool. That's a bold position for two founders whose backgrounds are data protection and privacy law. How do we say, out loud, that Prefera is ending the era of inferred targeting, without it sounding like one more compliance product?"

### S04 Vision & Ambition: The 5 Year Horizon

Baseline: In five years Prefera has moved through every phase you've mapped, from "How do I want to be contacted?" to "What am I currently looking for or open to?" The communication preference service has become a user-controlled Digital Persona, Saudi Arabia is the base for the wider region, and Prefera is the preference infrastructure organizations check against by default: the place an Opportunity gets matched to a Persona without the profile ever being exposed.

Steering Prompt: "Your brief describes four phases, and Majed, on the kick-off you said there will be many more than three. Five years from now, is Prefera a utility that every Saudi bank and telco quietly checks against, or a consumer brand people actively choose, the way they choose Spotify? Which is the bigger ambition, and does this brand need to carry both?"

### S05 Vision & Ambition: The Internal Mandate

Baseline: Nothing reaches a person that they haven't asked for, and every verification can be proven afterwards. The daily directive is to translate legal requirements into technical requirements so cleanly that the product stays simple for the individual and practical for the organization.

Steering Prompt: "Majed, you described what you love as translating legal requirements into technical requirements. Is that the directive the whole team works to every single day? And when Prefera has fifty people and neither of you can review every release, how do you protect it?"

### S06 Vision & Ambition: The External Pitch

Baseline: To deliver [a shared communication preference network] for [both sides of every marketing message] so they can [replace unwanted outreach with wanted interactions].

Steering Prompt: "Let's validate this sentence: we deliver a shared communication preference network for both sides of every marketing message, so they can replace unwanted outreach with wanted interactions. Does that capture your complete value, or does it undersell the Digital Persona you're building towards?"

### S07 Core Beliefs: The Non-Negotiables

Baseline: One, verify before contact: no organization reaches a person without checking their current preference. Two, every check leaves a point-in-time record, ready for an audit or a complaint. Three, preferences are declared, never inferred: no tracking, no profiling. Four, interests are never sold and the complete Persona is never exposed. Five, simple first: if a screen needs explaining, it isn't finished.

Steering Prompt: "Your values include Transparency and Accountability, which almost every privacy company claims. The behaviour behind them is far sharper: point-in-time verification records, and never exposing the complete profile. Are those rules you'd hold even if a large bank offered to pay more for raw data?"

### S08 The Scaling Mantra

Baseline: Declared, not inferred. Whatever Prefera becomes, the person tells Prefera what they want. Prefera never guesses.

Steering Prompt: "Today the app opens with one switch: I don't like to receive any calls or messages. That's the person fully in control. At ten times the size, with brands and Opportunities in the mix, is that the thing you'd fight hardest to protect, the person always holding the switch?"

### S09 The Moat & The Pillars

Baseline: The Moat is a two-sided network effect. Every person who declares preferences makes Prefera more valuable to every organization, and every organization that verifies makes declaring worth it. A single-brand consent tool can't copy that without becoming a neutral network, which is a different business. The Pillars: one Digital Persona per person, shared across organizations; three verification routes through single search, bulk check and API; point-in-time verification records; and an Arabic-first product built for the Saudi regulatory landscape by a privacy lawyer and a data-protection engineer.

Steering Prompt: "If Didomi announced tomorrow a consumer app with a shared preference network for Saudi Arabia, what would they be missing that you already have? Is the real moat the network itself, the Arabic-first experience, or the two of you?"

### S10 The Onlyness Statement

Baseline: Prefera is the ONLY [consumer-led preference network] that provides [high-volume Saudi organizations] with [preferences declared by people themselves] in an era where [inferred targeting is losing people's trust]. Tension to flag: the claim holds as a shared, multi-organization network in Saudi Arabia, but the network doesn't exist until launch, and a privacy lawyer may resist an absolute claim.

Steering Prompt: "Here's the stake in the ground: Prefera is the only consumer-led preference network that provides high-volume Saudi organizations with preferences declared by people themselves, in an era where inferred targeting is losing people's trust. Nouf, as a lawyer, would you sign off on the word only? If not, what would we soften, or what proof would make it bulletproof?"

### S11 Brand Perception ("The Scenario")

Baseline: Your three words are Respectful, Intelligent and Empowering. What you want to overhear is the feeling behind them: they actually asked me first; it just works; I'm the one in charge of this.

Steering Prompt: "You gave us Respectful, Intelligent and Empowering. Picture someone in a café in Riyadh telling a friend about Prefera, completely unprompted. What do they actually say? And what does a marketing manager at a bank say about you to a colleague? Are those the same three feelings?"

## Section 2: Alignment & Audience

### S12 The Target Persona

FOR PEOPLE (PREFERA APP)

Baseline: Digitally active, 18 to 40, in Saudi Arabia, actively hunting for offers and digital services, and already expecting personalization from every app they use. Their mindset: I don't mind hearing from brands. I mind hearing from the wrong ones, at the wrong time.

Steering Prompt: "Your consumer is 18 to 40 and actively searches for offers. That's not someone who hates marketing. Is the person you're picturing the one who switches everything off, or the one who wants the Founding Day deal from the brands they love, and nothing else?"

FOR ORGANIZATIONS (PREFERA BUSINESS)

Baseline: The person who owns outbound campaigns or customer communications at a bank, telco, insurer, retailer or e-commerce platform with a large customer base. A CRM or marketing lead under pressure to hit campaign numbers without complaints or regulatory exposure, with compliance or data protection as the likely co-signer.

Steering Prompt: "On the business side, who physically signs the subscription: the head of marketing who wants better response rates, or the data protection officer who wants the audit trail? Their first objection will be completely different, so which one are we designing the brand for?"

### S13 Pain Points & Triggers

FOR PEOPLE (PREFERA APP)

Baseline: The pain is a phone full of promotional calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages they never agreed to, and no single place to make it stop. The trigger is the irrelevant call that arrives at exactly the wrong moment.

Steering Prompt: "What's the moment someone in Riyadh actually downloads Prefera? Is it one bad experience, a call at the wrong hour, the tenth message that week, or does it take a big season like Saudi National Day to bring people in?"

FOR ORGANIZATIONS (PREFERA BUSINESS)

Baseline: The pain is marketing spend wasted on the wrong person, channel or time, plus complaints, reputational damage and regulatory exposure. The trigger is either a complaint they couldn't prove they'd handled properly, or a large seasonal campaign about to go out to a big list.

Steering Prompt: "You've named Back to School, Saudi National Day and Founding Day as commercial moments. For an organization, is the buying trigger the campaign that's coming up, or the complaint that just landed? Which one gets a budget signed this week?"

### S14 The "Aha!" Moment

FOR PEOPLE (PREFERA APP)

Baseline: The quiet week after setting preferences, when the only messages that arrive are the ones they asked for. Or the first time they open History and see exactly which organizations checked their number.

Steering Prompt: "In the app, History shows a person which organizations have checked their number. That could be the moment someone realises Prefera is working for them. Is that the Aha, or is it the quiet week afterwards when the noise stops?"

FOR ORGANIZATIONS (PREFERA BUSINESS)

Baseline: The first bulk check on a real campaign list, when they see how many contacts don't want to hear from them on that channel at that time, and realise they've just saved the spend and the complaints. The second Aha is answering a complaint with a point-in-time verification record in hand.

Steering Prompt: "Picture a telco uploading its list before a Founding Day campaign. What do they see on the results screen that makes them think: this has already paid for itself?"

### S15 Buyer Journey & Qualification

Baseline: The subscription is priced on the volume of numbers an organization wants to verify. Organizations qualify in with a large customer base, high outbound volume and a regulated sector. The path is register a business account, verify through single search, bulk check or API, then communicate accordingly. Minimum volumes, price points and launch targets haven't been shared yet.

Steering Prompt: "You've told us the subscription is based on the volume of numbers an organization wants to check. What's the smallest organization worth signing? And what does success look like six months after launch: how many organizations, how many people with preferences set? That number tells us how loud this brand needs to be."

### S16 The Brand Ecosystem

FOR PEOPLE (PREFERA APP)

Baseline: The Prefera app lets a person choose whether they're contacted at all, about what (advertisements, exclusive offers, service notifications), through which channel (calls, SMS, WhatsApp), on which days and at what times, with temporary preferences for set periods and a History of changes and checks.

Steering Prompt: "Today the app is about communication preferences. When the Digital Persona arrives, does it live inside the Prefera app, or does it become a name in its own right? We need to know now, because the logo has to make room for it."

FOR ORGANIZATIONS (PREFERA BUSINESS)

Baseline: Prefera Business offers three verification routes: single search, bulk check and API integration, each producing a verification record. Future layers add Organizations and Brands, Products and Services, then Intent and Opportunities.

Steering Prompt: "So we have Prefera for people and Prefera Business for organizations, under one brand. Is that architecture final, or do you see the business side eventually needing its own identity once Opportunities become a product organizations pay for?"

### S17 Routing & Triggers

FOR PEOPLE (PREFERA APP)

Baseline: The fork is between the person who flips the master switch to stop everything, and the person who sets granular preferences because they still want the right offers. The brand has to welcome both without judging either.

Steering Prompt: "When someone opens the app, they can switch everything off in one tap, or spend two minutes setting exactly what they want. Which behaviour does Prefera want to encourage, and does the brand ever nudge someone away from switching everything off?"

FOR ORGANIZATIONS (PREFERA BUSINESS)

Baseline: Green light for the API is outreach running continuously from a CRM or dialler, checking before every contact. Bulk check suits campaign-led teams sending periodic lists. Single search suits small teams and one-off checks.

Steering Prompt: "A mid-sized e-commerce brand runs a campaign every few weeks from a spreadsheet. A bank runs outbound calls all day from its CRM. What's the signal you listen for that says: stop, you don't need bulk checks, you need the API? And is there anyone you'd turn away?"

### S18 The Conversion Goal

Baseline: On the business platform the number one action is Sign Up for a business account, with How It Works as the secondary route. In the app it's entering a mobile number and tapping Set My Preferences in the first session. There's no capture mechanism yet for an organization that isn't ready to sign.

Steering Prompt: "On the business site the main button is Sign Up. For a bank's marketing director who isn't ready to commit, what's the fallback: a demo, a free sample check of part of their own list, or a conversation with you? And in the app, is the goal simply that every new user sets their preferences before they close it?"

## Section 3: Narrative & Voice

### S19 The Messaging Tree

Baseline: The Core (Why): every interaction should be one the person actually wants. The Pillars (How): declared, not inferred; verify before contact; one Persona across every organization; proof in every record. The Flex (What): to people, you decide who contacts you, how and when. To organizations, reach the right person, through the right channel, at the right time, and prove it.

Steering Prompt: "With a 25-year-old in Riyadh the pitch is control. Across the table from a bank's head of marketing it's efficiency and fewer complaints. Is that one story told twice, or are there moments where what's right for the person costs the organization a sale? How should the brand handle that tension?"

### S20 Tone of Voice

Baseline: Formal to Informal: centre, slightly informal in the app, slightly formal in Business. Understated to Bold: understated. Enthusiastic to Matter of Fact: matter of fact. Authoritative to Conversational: conversational, with quiet authority. Disruptive to Established: leaning established. Descriptive to Minimalist: minimalist. Creative to Engineered: engineered. Accessible to Premium: accessible, with Apple-level polish. Community-Led to Exclusive: community-led.

Steering Prompt: "Two of these scales feel contested. Disruptive or Established: you want to end inferred targeting, which is disruptive, but trust usually comes from an established voice. And Formal or Informal: a lawyer and an engineer will naturally lean formal, but your consumer is 18 to 40. Where do you want to land on those two?"

### S21 Voice Mechanics (The Hard Rules)

Baseline: The app speaks in first person on its main control ("I don't like to receive any calls or messages") and second person elsewhere ("You have not yet specified your communication preferences"). The business site uses third person ("Prefera enables businesses..."). Headlines and buttons use Title Case ("Set My Preferences", "Communicate with Preference."). Headlines take full stops. No emoji. Every screen has an English and Arabic version.

Steering Prompt: "Your app speaks in the person's own voice on the main switch, but the business site speaks about Prefera in the third person. Is that a deliberate split, the person's voice for people and Prefera's voice for businesses? And should Arabic always lead, with English following?"

### S22 Voice Mechanics (Power Words & Forbidden List)

Baseline (Power Words): Preference. Wanted, as in wanted interactions. Right person, right channel, right time. Verify. Persona.

Baseline (Kill List): Consent management. Tracking, targeting and profiling. Legalistic terms in consumer copy. Opt-out. Generic tech filler such as innovative, seamless and cutting-edge.

Steering Prompt: "Your brief says the brand must never feel restrictive, legalistic or surveillance-oriented, yet the category speaks in consent, compliance and targeting. Which of those words are you happy to ban outright? And how do you feel about control: it's in your values, but your personality section warns against looking restrictive. Is it a word you love, or one that makes you nervous?"

### S24 The Core Hooks

Baseline: "From unwanted marketing to wanted interactions." "Communicate with Preference." "Right person, right channel, right time." Majed's matchmaking analogy, and the name as a preference era.

Steering Prompt: "Majed, on the kick-off you said the name signals a new era, a preference era. That's a hook nobody else can use. ## Bespoke slides

### B01 Product Evolution: The Digital Persona

Baseline: The Digital Persona is the architecture from day one, not a later feature: One Person, One Digital Persona, different preferences, contexts and modes. Phase 1 is Communications, Phase 2 Organizations and Brands, Phase 3 Products and Services, Phase 4 Intent and Opportunities.

Steering Prompt: "Majed, you asked for the chance to walk us through the phases. Take us beyond the four in the brief. For each one, what's the single change the brand has to be ready for, so we don't design a logo that only works for Phase 1?"

### B02 Visual Direction: The Mark

Baseline: Simple and straightforward, unique without being abstract, and able to work at app icon size and at large scale. One mark across the app and Prefera Business, with room to stretch into the Digital Persona. Today the app is coral and the business platform is blue, both carrying a P in a rounded square.

Steering Prompt: "Right now the app uses coral and the business platform uses blue, both with a P in a rounded square. Is that split intentional, warm for people and cool for business, or something the developer chose? And Majed, you mentioned a lesson you learned from the last logo we worked on together. What would you do differently this time?"

### B03 Typography: Arabic First

Baseline: Arabic leads and English follows. The type has to work across the app, the business platform, the website, presentations and future marketing. At MVP the vendor hard-codes fonts, so licensing for app and web embedding, and a clear developer handover, matter as much as the look.

Steering Prompt: "How is Prefera written in Arabic, and is the Arabic wordmark the primary logo with English as its partner? Your developer hard-codes the fonts, so do we need a typeface that's free to embed in the app and on the web, or is there room for a licensed family?"
